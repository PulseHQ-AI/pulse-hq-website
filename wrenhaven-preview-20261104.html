The Western Dock Dispute Continues

Arthur Pike and ferry captain Mara Venn remain divided over the future of the western dock. Pike maintains the dock remains suitable for limited private use. Venn disagrees. "We're not waiting for somebody to fall through it before we do something," Venn told the Weekly. Local mechanic June Mercer has expressed interest in acquiring the adjoining workshop, provided the matter can be resolved. Council will hear public submissions on Tuesday evening.

Gull & Lantern Plans Live Music Nights

Daniel Venn has announced preliminary plans to reopen the pub's upstairs room for community music events. The room, little used for several years, reportedly contains an old upright piano. Schoolteacher Clara Finch has expressed interest in helping organise an opening evening. Whether the piano works remains a matter of some debate. A planning meeting will be held at the Gull & Lantern on Thursday.

Local Photographer Announces Archive Project

Owen Shaw is collecting photographs documenting the town's history and welcomes contributions from residents. One recent submission has raised questions about the date and location of a community gathering. Shaw declined to elaborate.

Post Office Seeks Historical Records Volunteers

Postmistress Ruth Calder is seeking volunteers to assist with the Harbour Hall archive restoration. The collection includes old photographs, council records and several incomplete family registers. "We've got boxes going back generations," Calder said. "Somebody ought to make sense of them before the mice do." Several documents, she notes, have proved difficult to reconcile with existing town records. Volunteers can register at the post office.

Former Resident Returns

A former Wrenhaven resident has returned to the community after several years away. Friends and acquaintances are reportedly delighted to see them back.

Garden Notes

Agnes Vale reports that the community garden's early spring planting is progressing well. She would particularly like people to stop walking through the bean beds. She knows who you are.

Ferry Service Reminder — The M.V. June Bell continues to operate on its normal schedule. Passengers are reminded to check their belongings before disembarking.
Community Supper — Sunday — Six o'clock at Harbour Hall. All residents welcome. Please bring something to share. Seating arrangements will be made by the volunteers.
Small Notices — Found: a brass key, near the eastern boat ramp; claim from Owen at Mercer Marine Repairs. Lost: Daniel's patience with the weather, last seen sometime in November.

The Weather

Rain expected through the weekend, with occasional breaks in the cloud. Fishermen are advised to watch the western inlet. Ferry service operating normally.

A Note from the Editor

The Weekly has received several enquiries concerning rumours circulating about residents returning to Wrenhaven after extended absences. We have no evidence of anything unusual occurring. People change. Memories fade. Small communities sometimes make more of things than they should. We would ask residents to exercise consideration before repeating stories about their neighbours.

New Arrival at Wrenhaven Harbour

The M.V. June Bell arrived shortly after eleven on Friday morning. There was nothing particularly unusual about the crossing. A little rain. Some wind through the inlet. A delayed departure caused by loading difficulties at the previous stop.

Among the passengers was a newcomer carrying a suitcase and a few personal belongings. Nobody in Wrenhaven knew him. Mara Venn helped unload the luggage before returning to the wheelhouse.

The newcomer stood for a moment on the wooden pier, looking towards the town. Across the road, Elsie Bell was opening the general store. Daniel was carrying a delivery into the Gull & Lantern. Somewhere further up the hill, a dog was barking. The rain had almost stopped.

The newcomer checked the address written on a folded piece of paper. Lantern Road. He had somewhere to stay. He had no idea what sort of place he'd arrived in. And Wrenhaven, for its part, had no idea what to make of him.

Around Town

The harbour & pier — where the June Bell lands and the town begins. The western dock — old, contested, and due before council. Mercer Marine Repairs — June's workshop, and the workshop she hopes will be hers. The general store — Elsie's; groceries, gossip, and keys to rented houses. The post office — Ruth's counter, and behind it, boxes of the town's memory. The Gull & Lantern — Daniel's pub; the upstairs room sleeps, for now. Harbour Hall — suppers, meetings, archives, and one chair nobody sits in. The community garden — Agnes's kingdom; mind the bean beds. Lantern Road — your house; and at the far end, a small cottage standing empty, its TO LET notice faded, waiting for nobody in particular.

Your Arrival — How to Live Here

You are the newcomer. You have a suitcase, a folded piece of paper with an address on it, and a house waiting on Lantern Road. The key is at the general store, with Elsie.

First, give him a name. Your character is yours — name him, and tell the town where he's from as you meet people. What you say about him becomes true here.

To do anything, just say it in our chat, the way you'd say it at a table: "I head up to the general store and introduce myself to Elsie." Residents answer in character, out of their own lives — and they remember what you say. Promises count. So do accusations.

The town lives in weeks. Each new edition of the Weekly reports what actually happened — your doings, and everyone else's. Quiet weeks happen. Real towns have them.

Your first afternoon is waiting: the pier, the rain letting up, and a short walk to the general store, where a woman who has seen a thousand arrivals is watching you come up the road.
